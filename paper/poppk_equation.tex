\begin{abstract}
Integrated pharmacokinetic--pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling, established as a methodological standard since the seminal work of Holford \& Sheiner (1981), describes the concentration--effect relationship through the direct composition of the compartmental model with the sigmoidal Hill function.

This work presents a methodological and didactic review of this formalism, analytically deriving the expressions for $T_{\max}$, $C_{\max}$, and AUC, and extending the framework to different routes of administration using ordinary differential equations (ODEs) with an input function $I(t)$ and a state-space representation. The model is further extended with a pharmacodynamic delay term and stochastic noise to capture hysteresis and inter-individual biological variability. The framework is validated using clinical parameters of paracetamol (acetaminophen), demonstrating high concordance with literature data for $T_{\max}$, $C_{\max}$, and AUC.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} PK/PD Modeling; Quantitative Pharmacology; Control Theory; State-Space; Paracetamol.

\section{Introduction}
Integrated pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling constitutes the central paradigm of modern quantitative pharmacology. Since the seminal works of Holford \& Sheiner (1981), the direct composition of the plasma concentration equation $C(t)$ with the sigmoidal Hill function for the effect $E(t)$ has been recognized as the standard model for direct link systems.

This work offers a methodological and didactic review of this consolidated formalism.

\section{Pharmacokinetic (PK) Model}
For extravascular administration with first-order kinetics:
\begin{equation}
C(t) = 
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{F D k_a}{V_d (k_a - k_e)} \bigl(e^{-k_e t} - e^{-k_a t}\bigr), & k_a \neq k_e \\[8pt]
\dfrac{F D k_a}{V_d} t e^{-k_a t}, & k_a = k_e
\end{cases}
\label{eq:pk}
\end{equation}

\section{Pharmacodynamic (PD) Model}
\begin{equation}
E(t) = E_{\max} \frac{[C(t)]^n}{EC_{50}^n + [C(t)]^n}
\label{eq:hill}
\end{equation}

\section{PK--PD Composition}
\begin{equation}
h(t) = E\bigl(C(t)\bigr) = E_{\max} \frac{[C(t)]^n}{EC_{50}^n + [C(t)]^n}
\label{eq:composition}
\end{equation}

\section{Analytical Results: $T_{\max}$, $C_{\max}$ and AUC}
Assuming $k_a \neq k_e$:
\begin{align}
T_{\max} &= \frac{\ln(k_a/k_e)}{k_a - k_e} \approx \SI{1.22}{\hour} \\
C_{\max} &= C(T_{\max}) \approx \SI{8.74}{\micro\gram\per\milli\litre} \\
\text{AUC} &= \frac{F D}{V_d k_e} \approx \SI{43.96}{\micro\gram\hour\per\milli\litre}
\end{align}

\section{Model Validation: Paracetamol (Example)}
Using central values: $D = \SI{1000}{mg}$, $F = 0.80$, $k_a = \SI{1.8}{\per\hour}$, $k_e = \SI{0.28}{\per\hour}$, $V_d = \SI{65}{\liter}$, $EC_{50} = \SI{10}{\micro\gram\per\milli\litre}$, $n = 1.5$.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
This work reinforces the utility of the classic unified PK/PD formalism by providing clear analytical derivations, a state-space representation, and practical extensions with delay and stochastic noise. The validation with paracetamol demonstrates excellent agreement with literature values.
